# The DP-made color/camera app (Chromagraph), legitimate look replication, and AI color tools (as of Nov 2026)

Research note scope: (1) identify the app the user remembers as "Chromacast" by the DP of "Knives Out"; (2) how its look, or any color pipeline's look, can legitimately be replicated on other cameras (Sony FX3, Canon Legria FS19); (3) AI/ML color tools for indie filmmakers in 2025-2026.

## 1. What is the app? Name, creator, platform, price, launch, and how its pipeline works

### Takeaway
The app is **Chromagraph Camera** (often shortened to "Chromagraph"), not "Chromacast". No app called "Chromacast" turned up. Steve Yedlin, ASC (DP of all three Knives Out films) designed it with director Rian Johnson and color-science programmer Eric Cameron. It is sold by Chromatic Applications LLC. It is a $12.99 one-time-purchase, **stills-only** iPhone camera app. It went live on the App Store on Oct 20, 2026 (one source says Oct 22) and reached #1 in Photo & Video. It captures raw sensor data, skips Apple's look processing, and applies one fixed, curated Yedlin look, including grain and halation. It exports only JPEG/HEIC with the look baked in. It has no LUT export and no video. I found no evidence that it processes images it did not capture itself.

**How the pipeline works (creator and developer statements)**
- "Not a filter": a filter "sits on top of the processing your phone has already done". Chromagraph "starts from raw sensor data and builds the image its own way… a different process from the start". — [Chromatic Applications, Chromagraph page](https://chromaticapplications.com/chromagraph/chromagraph.html)
- Yedlin: the app "completely bypasses the iPhone look management and replaces it with the same custom math I use on all my films, tailored to Apple camera behavior." He calls the look "opinionated": "the nuanced and idiosyncratic rendering of tonality and chromaticity (as well as grain and halation)". There are no film-stock presets, only one core look that the user adjusts. — [CineD quoting Yedlin's Instagram post](https://www.cined.com/steve-yedlin-and-rian-johnson-release-chromagraph-camera-film-color-pipeline-for-iphone-stills-grain-halation-and-a-raw-gallery/)
- Yedlin on iPhone raw: "The code values themselves… alone don't tell you how bright something's supposed to be. It's the code values in the pixels plus this [metadata] evaluation… It's not like we're putting different parameters into existing processing math. It's totally different math for the most part." iPhone sensors "spread image information out in a different way from how film cameras work." — [IndieWire](https://www.indiewire.com/features/craft/steve-yedlin-interview-chromagraph-camera-app-rian-johnson-1235225234/)
- Philosophy: good images are not "photometrically or colorimetrically 'correct'". Even a broadcast look is "not correct in a different way". Yedlin calls it "generally artful rendering", not a "Knives Out look": "This is just the image processing… the image processing part isn't the only thing that makes a photo." He says it particularly helps with vibrant colors, avoiding the "saturation clamped look where one of the three channels is slammed". The user-facing tools are "meant to be exactly like the math of white balance and ISO on a film camera" (IndieWire's paraphrase). — [IndieWire](https://www.indiewire.com/features/craft/steve-yedlin-interview-chromagraph-camera-app-rian-johnson-1235225234/)
- Workflow: you frame with a non-looked viewfinder ("Shoot first, see the look after"). The gallery stores **full-quality scene-linear raw originals**, applies the look and adjustments live and non-destructively, and exports smaller JPEG/HEIC files with the look baked in. Optional auto-export to the Photos library. — [CineD](https://www.cined.com/steve-yedlin-and-rian-johnson-release-chromagraph-camera-film-color-pipeline-for-iphone-stills-grain-halation-and-a-raw-gallery/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chromagraph-camera/id6803329435)
- The editor has four sections: Tone, Color, Texture and Crop. Texture has separate Grain and Halation tabs. Grain sliders cover amplitude and grain size, plus a "Force Pixel Size Grain" switch. The editor shows metadata (shutter, aperture, ISO, WB in Kelvin). The developer publishes no supported-model list and does not say which raw format is recorded. — [CineD](https://www.cined.com/steve-yedlin-and-rian-johnson-release-chromagraph-camera-film-color-pipeline-for-iphone-stills-grain-halation-and-a-raw-gallery/)
- User reviews report no sharpening ("output currently appears muddy"), inconsistent results, and skin-tone issues in some shots. — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chromagraph-camera/id6803329435)
- Intellectual background: Yedlin's 2016 essay argues that "the substantial part of the perceptual look comes from the mathematical preparation of the image for display and not from the camera format". He limits this to high-quality formats, "not inferior acquisition devices like, say smartphone cameras". He describes his tools as: empirical tone mapping; 3D color-space geometry; **scattered data interpolation** that maps measured Alexa data onto Kodak 5219 film data; a probability-based (not geometry-based) grain model; and empirical halation and gate-weave models. — [Yedlin, "On Color Science for Filmmakers"](http://www.yedlin.net/NerdyFilmTechStuff/OnColorScience/index.html)

## 2. How can a look like this (or any color pipeline's) legitimately be extracted and applied to other cameras?

### Takeaway
The standard legitimate method is black-box observation: put known inputs through the pipeline, record the outputs, and fit a 3D LUT (or a smoother scattered-data model) mapping one to the other. For a LUT or editor this is easy, using a HALD/identity image with tools like G'MIC, lut-estimator, clut, two_img_lut, 3D LUT Creator, MatchLight or colour-science. Chromagraph is harder: it accepts only its own raw captures and exports baked JPEG/HEIC. The inputs therefore have to be **physical scenes and charts** shot in parallel with a reference capture, and grain and halation have to be disabled or modelled separately, because a LUT can only capture per-pixel ("intrapixel") behavior.

### Cited Findings
**Yedlin's own framework (useful vocabulary and limits)**
- Yedlin splits look attributes into three groups. **Intrapixel** covers contrast, density, color rendition and highlight rolloff. **Spatial** covers resolution, sharpness and halation. **Temporal** covers motion blur and shutter. Grain and gate weave straddle categories. He built his film-matching model by mapping two measured datasets (Alexa vs scanned Kodak 5219) with tone mapping plus scattered data interpolation. — [Yedlin, On Color Science](http://www.yedlin.net/NerdyFilmTechStuff/OnColorScience/index.html)
- He holds that conventional color grading math is "too primitive and simple" to author such transforms. Grading is a "polishing step" on top of the core display-prep transform. — [Yedlin, On Color Science](http://www.yedlin.net/NerdyFilmTechStuff/OnColorScience/index.html)
- Capture data limits what can be matched: "Recovering data that was never captured is impossible. But what is possible is to remap captured data to our desired look." Choose the camera that collects more data (latitude, shutter), not the one that looks nicer out of the box. — [Yedlin, On Color Science](http://www.yedlin.net/NerdyFilmTechStuff/OnColorScience/index.html)

**Tools for fitting a LUT from input/output pairs**
- **ImageMagick / HALD workflow:** create a Hald identity image, process it the same way as your image, then apply the result with `-hald-clut` to any other image. This works only where the tool accepts arbitrary images as input. — [ImageMagick forum](https://legacy.imagemagick.org/discourse-server/viewtopic.php?p=126148)
- **G'MIC "CLUT from After/Before Layers"** (since G'MIC 2.3.4) recovers the transform between a before/after pair as a HaldCLUT. — [Natron/G'MIC docs](https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.6/_sources/plugins/eu.gmic.CLUTfromAfterBeforeLayers.rst.txt); [pixls.us thread](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/how-to-create-haldclut-from-difference-between-two-images/12651)
- **lut-estimator** (PyPI) samples aligned before/after pairs, reconstructs a 3D LUT and exports a `.cube` file. — [PyPI lut-estimator](https://pypi.org/project/lut-estimator/)
- **clut** (GitHub, oiao) fits a HaldCLUT from one or more (unfiltered, filtered) tuples with identical composition. — [github.com/oiao/clut](https://github.com/oiao/clut)
- **darktable_lut_generator** builds a LUT from RAW plus out-of-camera JPEG pairs (a directory of pairs with a wide range of colors). This mirrors the "raw in, baked output out" situation of a camera app. — [socket.dev / PyPI darktable-lut-generator](https://socket.dev/pypi/package/darktable-lut-generator/overview/0.1.4)
- **two_img_lut** (open source) generates a `.cube` from two same-size images. **MatchLight** (Light Illusion) generates LUTs from two or more images. **Briz Colors Matcher** creates a 3D LUT matching one image to a reference. — [awesome.ecosyste.ms two_img_lut](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fpinaki82%2Ftwo_img_lut); [Light Illusion MatchLight](https://www.lightillusion.com/matchlight.htm); [Softpedia Briz Colors Matcher](https://www.softpedia.com/get/Multimedia/Graphic/Graphic-Others/Briz-Colors-Matcher.shtml)
- **3D LUT Creator** has a Color Match tool that copies toning from a reference image. Fstoppers found it works very well but sometimes needs manual work. Price was quoted at $249 (possibly outdated). — [lutcreator.com tutorials](https://lutcreator.com/3d-lut-creator---tutorials.html); [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/post-production/new-quick-and-easy-way-make-lut-any-image-246170)
- Camera-to-camera matching with a generated LUT is a long-established workflow. — [4kshooters, 2016](https://4kshooters.net/2016/06/29/generate-a-lut-to-match-colours-between-two-different-cameras-with-this-simple-workflow)
- **colour-science (Python)** reads and writes LUT1D, LUT3x1D and LUT3D, including Iridas `.cube` (`read_LUT` / `write_LUT`). The separate BSD-3 **colour-checker-detection** package locates ColorChecker charts in images. Together they form a free toolkit for chart-based fitting. The search did not surface a ready-made combined "chart to LUT" workflow. — [colour.io.luts docs](https://colour.readthedocs.io/en/develop/_modules/colour/io/luts.html); [colour-checker-detection on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/colour-checker-detection/)
- Precedent: Sony's published LUTs were reportedly reverse-engineered from Sony RAW Viewer. — [The Cerberus Project](https://jordanwesthoff.gitbooks.io/the-cerberus-project/chapter_8_3.html)

**Spatial and texture effects are separate tools in the professional market**
- ARRI Film Lab 1.5 adds grain and halation in post (8mm–65mm formats, ACES 2.0). CineD links it as the pro-side analogue to Chromagraph's Texture tab. Colourlab's Astra Film likewise combines "film colour, grain, lens simulation, halation and spatial effects". — [CineD article (related link)](https://www.cined.com/steve-yedlin-and-rian-johnson-release-chromagraph-camera-film-color-pipeline-for-iphone-stills-grain-halation-and-a-raw-gallery/); [Colourlab Ai 4](https://colourlab.ai/products/colourlab-ai-4/)

**Legal and ethical boundaries**
- Apple's Standard EULA bans reverse-engineering, disassembling, deriving source code of, or creating derivative works of the app. — [Apple Standard EULA](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/dev/stdeula/)
- Contract terms can restrict use beyond what copyright alone would. *Bowers v. Baystate* (Fed. Cir.) upheld contractual limits on reverse engineering. — [SMU Computer Law Review note](https://scholar.smu.edu/comtech/vol8/iss3/7)
- Commercial LUT licenses commonly forbid redistributing the LUT, even modified. Whether LUTs are copyrightable is unsettled. A pixls.us developer wrote a custom license to address the "gray area". — [pixls.us spektrafilm LUTs thread](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/spektrafilm-luts/57879)
- Limited reverse engineering for interoperability has been allowed by courts. This comes from an informal source, not legal authority. — [Stanford CS181 project page](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/eroberts/cs181/projects/1998-99/copyright-infringement/revengillegal.html)

### Inferences
- **Practical recipe for a Chromagraph-like look on FX3 (conceptual, not tested):**
  1. Set Chromagraph's grain amplitude to zero and halation off (or minimal), and leave Tone/Color sliders at default, so the output is as close to purely intrapixel as possible.
  2. In controlled light, shoot a ColorChecker (ideally a larger chart such as a 140-patch or SG) plus gray-scale and exposure brackets. Capture it with Chromagraph, and also with the same iPhone in ProRAW/DNG (or a known linear raw) as the scene reference.
  3. Fit a mapping from the scene-linear reference to the Chromagraph output (tone curve plus 3D LUT, or RBF/thin-plate scattered interpolation as Yedlin describes), using colour-science or darktable_lut_generator-style tooling.
  4. Separately, bring the FX3 S-Log3/S-Gamut3.Cine into the same scene-linear space (via ACES or a known IDT). Then apply the fitted look and re-add grain and halation with dedicated tools (Resolve Film Look Creator or halation FX, Dehancer, ARRI Film Lab, Astra).
- The fitted result will be an approximation. Chromagraph applies **metadata-dependent** processing (per Yedlin, iPhone pixel values need metadata to know brightness), exposure-dependent behavior, and possibly gamut-mapping tied to Apple's sensor. A single static LUT cannot reproduce scene-adaptive or local tone mapping, grain, halation, or sharpening differences. JPEG/HEIC 8-bit output also limits fitting precision in the shadows and highlights.
- This may not be a viable path for a **Canon Legria FS19** (an older consumer camcorder with baked-in video gamma and 8-bit compressed recording). Yedlin himself says the look-transfer thesis assumes capture that "gets enough information", and missing latitude or gamut cannot be recovered. Expect only a loose approximation from that camera. The FX3 (log, 10-bit) is the realistic target.
- Ethically, the low-risk line is: (a) a personal or internal look built from your own test shots, (b) not decompiling the app, (c) not selling or distributing the fitted LUT as "the Yedlin/Chromagraph/Knives Out look", and (d) not using the creators' names in marketing. Fitting from photos you took is black-box observation of outputs, not disassembly of code. But the EULA's "derivative works… or any part thereof" wording makes this a gray area. This is not legal advice.
- An arguably cleaner path is to follow Yedlin's own method: measure, then author your own transform (or wait for the official OFX plugins) rather than cloning his.

### Gaps
- I found no published case of anyone extracting a Chromagraph LUT, and no community test of how well a LUT approximates it.
- I found no authoritative legal source on LUT copyrightability in US or EU law.
- It is unconfirmed whether Chromagraph's grain and halation can be fully disabled (amplitude to zero is implied by the sliders, not documented).
- I did not verify Lattice's or Resolve's own "generate LUT from image pair" capabilities in 2026 documentation.

## 3. AI/ML color tools and research relevant to indie filmmakers (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
What is useful today is **AI-assisted balancing and shot/reference matching** (Colourlab Ai 4, Resolve's Color Match and Shot Match) and **ML-assisted secondaries**. These produce editable nodes or LUT-like results that feed a normal grade. The research frontier (neural implicit LUTs, image-adaptive and spatially-aware LUTs, 4D LUT style transfer) explains where tools are heading but mostly isn't productized. Generative video-to-video restyling (Runway Aleph, Luma Modify) changes look, texture and content. It is not a controllable color pipeline, and it is risky for narrative continuity.

**Research: learned LUTs and color transfer**
- **NILUT** (Conde, Vazquez-Corral, Brown, Timofte; AAAI 2024): a neural network parameterizes a continuous 3D color transform. It can accurately emulate real 3D LUTs, is memory-efficient, and can blend multiple styles. — [arXiv 2306.11920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11920)
- **IA-NILUT** (2024): an image-adaptive, prompt-guided NILUT using five image-adaptive parameters, with "LUT bypassing" that converts the MLP into a LUT for per-pixel use. — [arXiv 2408.11055](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.11055)
- **NLUT** (2023): neural 3D LUTs for video photorealistic style transfer. — [arXiv 2303.09170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.09170)
- **SA-LUT** (2025): a spatially adaptive 4D LUT for photorealistic style transfer. It addresses the spatial limits of global 3D LUTs. — [arXiv 2506.13465](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13465)
- **Spatial-aware LUT decomposition** (2025): real-time image enhancement. It reports NILUT failed to fit image-adaptiveness in its comparison. — [arXiv 2508.16121](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16121v1)
- **Image-adaptive 3D LUTs** (Zeng et al., TPAMI 2020): the foundational line behind these papers. Known from background knowledge only; it did not surface in searches and was not verified this session.

### Inferences
- **Useful now for an FX3 + Legria indie workflow:**
  - Resolve (free or Studio) Color Match on a chart shot by both cameras, to normalize them before a look.
  - Resolve Shot Match and Magic Mask for continuity.
  - Colourlab Ai 4's reference matching and Auto Balance, if on Apple silicon and working from non-RAW media (FX3 XAVC S-Log3 qualifies; FX3 RAW over HDMI does not yet).
  - Classic chart-based LUT fitting (colour-science) for a "house look". This matches Yedlin's own measure-then-map philosophy.
- **Partly hype:** "one-click cinematic look" claims; reference-still matching for final creative grades (it is a starting point); generative restyling for narrative color work (it alters content and texture, is length-limited, isn't deterministic, and is hard to match shot-to-shot).
- Neural-LUT research (NILUT, SA-LUT) is the academic version of "learn a look from pairs". It suggests that future tools will fit a look from a few reference pairs with spatial awareness, which addresses the grain/halation/local-contrast gap a 3D LUT has. Today it is code-on-GitHub level for most indies.
